# V0.3e — Lifecycle maintenance and compatibility operations

**Release:** v8.58.0  
**Backlog:** B256–B265  
**Date:** 2026-07-29

## Outcome

V0.3e converts lifecycle readiness evidence into immutable maintenance and
campaign plans. Provider execution stays in the durable-operation subsystem:
this batch does not synthesize provider requests, invoke package managers,
apply firmware, reboot hosts, upgrade guests or renew certificates implicitly.

## Data model and permissions

Migration 132 is additive and creates eleven stores: maintenance plans and
waves, lifecycle campaigns and targets, live-patch evidence, reboot signals,
firmware catalog, driver compatibility, certificate ownership, reminder
policies and reminder instances. It adds `lifecycle_maintenance.manage` and
`lifecycle_certificates.manage` to the governance catalog and grants them to
the site-admin role. The initial service remains administrator-only.

Every flexible document is size-bounded and rejects secret-shaped fields.
References and names use restricted character sets, vendor sources require
credential-free HTTPS, exact records are unique, and payloads receive stable
SHA-256 hashes where evidence identity matters.

## Maintenance and campaign behavior

The planner greedily packs targets into bounded waves while keeping the same
availability group out of one wave and enforcing maximum concurrency per
owner. Required evacuation that is unavailable or unverified and a duration
shorter than the estimated wave total become explicit conflicts. Only a ready,
conflict-free plan can be approved with its exact hash and typed phrase.

Cluster, guest-tools and VM-hardware campaigns have distinct gates. Approval
does not create operations. Advancement accepts only the current stage and an
existing provider-operation ID. The operation must match the target host, be
`succeeded`, and accompany positive post-verification evidence; otherwise the
target fails and the campaign pauses. This keeps adapter-specific execution,
retry, locks and reconciliation in the established durable engine.

## Live patch, reboot and compatibility behavior

Live-patch providers are injected adapters. Inventory can run independently;
apply and verify additionally need an approved `live_patch.apply` record whose
payload hash binds provider, host, target, patch and operation, plus the exact
typed confirmation. Adapter output is evidence-only and cannot infer a reboot.

Reboot signals are independently upserted by source and aggregate fail-safe:
any required signal wins, otherwise unknown wins over not-required. Firmware
and driver data are exact, source-digested catalog records. They provide
compatibility guidance and never invoke a vendor tool or remediation action.

## Certificate behavior

Ownership can reference the existing tracked-certificate inventory or register
a resource awaiting discovery. It records endpoint, service or host ownership,
environment, escalation administrator and optional maintenance-plan dependency.
Reminder policies choose bounded day thresholds and environments. Evaluation
uses a uniqueness key across policy, ownership, threshold and expiry, so repeat
runs are idempotent and report `renewalsStarted: 0`.

## Control surface and verification

Governance → Lifecycle & updates exposes planners, campaign approvals and
advancement, live-patch/reboot evidence, firmware and driver catalog forms,
certificate ownership and reminder evaluation. Mutating controls are audited.

The dedicated 12-test suite covers the eleven exact stores, permissions and
role grants; wave/conflict/approval behavior; all campaign kinds and pause
semantics; supported/unsupported live patch; reboot aggregation; official
firmware and exact driver matching; certificate joins; and idempotent reminders.
